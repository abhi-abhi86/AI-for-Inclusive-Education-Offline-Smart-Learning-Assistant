Photosynthesis is the fundamental biological process by which green plants, algae, and certain bacteria transform light energy into chemical energy. During this process, plants absorb sunlight using a green pigment called chlorophyll, which is located inside cell structures termed chloroplasts. 

Plants take in carbon dioxide from the surrounding air through tiny microscopic pores in their leaves known as stomata, while simultaneously absorbing water and essential mineral nutrients from the soil through their root systems. Powered by the captured solar energy, a chemical reaction occurs inside the chloroplasts that converts carbon dioxide and water into glucose, a simple sugar that serves as primary fuel for plant growth, cellular repair, and reproduction. 

As an essential byproduct of this chemical synthesis, oxygen gas is released back into the atmosphere through the stomata. This liberated oxygen is vital for the survival of aerobic organisms on Earth, including humans and animals, who require oxygen for cellular respiration. Therefore, photosynthesis not only provides the organic biomass forming the base of almost all terrestrial and aquatic food chains, but it also maintains atmospheric oxygen balance and regulates planetary carbon dioxide levels.
